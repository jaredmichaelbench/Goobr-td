# Single-player journey: discussion

_2026-10-31 · agreed, recorded as D-0048 (Jared finished his markup 04:28Z). Claude Doc (live copy, mark it up there): https://claude.ai/artifact/X61gYWSz2iypqLEeqM2Eug_

Proposal: single player becomes a journey of song-length levels, each one full track (1:40 to 3:00), with building in the song's lulls, a rogue-lite card after each level, and talents still from XP. This replaces "clear a wave, build at leisure, press Start" with a run where the music sets the pace.

Nothing in the build changes until we agree (rule 6). Source: Jared's project-chat message of 2026-10-31 03:49Z ("I want to re-think the games single player...").

## 1. A level is one song

Recommend: yes. A level starts when the song starts and ends when it ends, and the enemies are written to the song's sections, not to a creep count. This is the "long scripted waves for single player" backlog idea, promoted.

- **Inside a level, waves follow the music.** A verse sends a steady stream, a chorus or drop sends the big push, a break is quiet. The song's waveform gives a first draft of where those are; each song is then hand-tuned.
- **The songs you have fit.** The 11 VOiD1 tracks run 1:28 to 2:57, about 2:05 on average. Two are under the 1:40 floor (No Pressure Trance 1:28, Psychedelia Flashback 1:36).
- **Boss levels can run long.** A multi-stage boss can loop a section or chain two tracks.
- **Today's waves become sections.** The 20-wave run is roughly 8 to 10 songs' worth of fighting.

Base template for a level, the "hero's journey" (Jared): it is the anchor: most levels play it straight so players learn and trust it; only then can a level break it on purpose for excitement and misdirection, and those breaks stay rare. Not to scale: Setup (fresh map, build) → Count-off or intro → Push (verse) → Lull (build) → Big push (chorus) → Lull (build) → Finale (drop or boss) → Card (pick 1 of 3).

## 2. Build in the lulls, and the count-off

Recommend: building is always allowed, before the song and during it, but the lulls are where it is safe. No "Start wave" button: the song is the clock.

- **Before the song.** Study the map as long as you like; Start level puts you on the clock. Setup length is to feel out in testing (Jared: 20 to 30 s with limited gold may feel like no pressure and bring back spawn crowding), so the setup IS the count-off (1, 2, 3, 4): slam down a few walls and a tower to protect the camp, and go; the start of a level is easy anyway (Jared).
- **Lulls are build windows.** A break spawns nothing new, so once the board is clear there is a short window, about 4 to 8 bars (roughly 8 to 16 s at 120 BPM), to build and upgrade: enough to breathe, never enough to build slowly (Jared); tuned in testing. Clear faster, get more of the window.
- **The count-off.** A song with no intro gets drumsticks 1, 2, 3, 4 and the first enemies are already walking, so a few towers near the king buy time while the maze goes up across the map. This is the fix for crowding the spawn.
- **Signalled, not guessed.** See open question 3.
- **Watch:** walls placed mid-push can abuse live rerouting. The always-leave-a-path rule holds; walls may also take a beat or two to raise during a push.

**Stopping path abuse: brainstorm** (Jared wants a clever fix; also likes Warcraft 3 style construction animations):

1. Construction time: walls and towers rise over a bar or two with an animation and only block once finished.
2. No placing on a tile a creep occupies or is about to enter.
3. Selling mid-push refunds less (e.g. 50% instead of 75%).
4. A creep whose path a new wall closes attacks that wall (Sapper idea, playtest 4).

Claude's lean: test 1 and 3 first.

## 3. A card after each level, talents from XP

Recommend: one draft (pick 1 of 3) after every level; talents unchanged, one point per XP level.

- **The count barely changes.** Today: eight drafts a 20-wave run (D-0040). An 8 to 10 song journey gives 8 to 10.
- **Different jobs.** The card is the big choice between levels; talents are the slow build spent in a lull.
- **Level-up timing fits.** Playtest 4 asked for the level-up chime to wait; here it plays at the next lull.
- **Boss levels** could offer a rarer draft or two picks.

## 4. Stages get harder

Recommend: length stays song-sized; density and the asks per bar rise. Each level adds one new thing.

- **Early:** creeps only, long lulls, short calls.
- **Middle:** elites in every push, shorter lulls, the first field bosses, and the first maps with more than one spawn to break up the campaign (Jared).
- **Late:** two or three field bosses in one song, elite packs in the lulls, new enemy types from the playtest 4 list.
- **Finale:** the campaign's last stage is its ultimate boss (below).
- **Beat switches** need a tempo map in the beat clock (today one fixed BPM per song, D-0010). Worth it, late in the plan.
- **"Lighting bosses":** read as a light show synced to the song; Jared to confirm.

**Two tiers of boss** (Jared's doc comment, 2026-10-31, like Lynels in Breath of the Wild):

- **Field bosses** are the top rank of elites, one or more mixed into a level's pushes, each with its own attack string and stagger and more XP. The elite roster grows into these.
- **Stage bosses** are placed by hand per campaign, not on a fixed rhythm (Jared, 04:30Z: e.g. level 5 gets a longer ~3:00 song and a strong boss); each takes over the whole song: a short opener, then the fight through every section, a new string each chorus, escorts only as part of its moves. Home of multi-stage fights and beat switches.
- **What it changes:** today's wave 5/10/15/20 bosses become stage bosses; Volley and Stomp can move to field bosses.

**The ultimate boss** (Jared): a long campaign ends on one epic track of about 5 minutes, still one song; a fight players hunt for and learn, replayable on its own with a best score once beaten. Needs a track found or commissioned (longest today is 2:57).

**Boss gimmicks** (Jared): every stage boss gets a name and one trick of its own. Brainstorm (working names):

- **The Peeker** (Jared): never walks the maze; leans in from the stage edge every few bars to fire, and while in view every note targets it.
- **The Tunneler:** burrows under the maze, surfaces somewhere new each chorus.
- **The Twins:** a stagger splits it in two, one answers Q and one E.
- **The Silencer:** mutes stems as it attacks; landed notes bring the music back.
- **The Rival:** a dark conductor stealing your towers' notes; land the call to win each phrase back.

**Bait and switch** (Jared's doc comment): some levels, mostly stage bosses, run longer than one song.

- **Multi-song levels:** a boss fight carried across two tracks, the switch as its phase change.
- **The fake ending:** the song ends, the results card starts, then a secret third phase or a short, insane extra segment.
- **Template first, then the twist:** once or twice a campaign, or players stop trusting endings.

## 5. Why you move: the theme

Recommend: stay the defender, with a pilgrimage or caravan. The king travels, and each level is a camp on the road that must hold for one song.

| Framing | The story | What you do | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pilgrimage (recommended) | The king and his band travel somewhere that matters; enemies hunt them on the road | Defend the camp for one song, move on | Keeps the whole core loop. Needs a reason to travel |
| Foothold | You are the invader; each map is ground you just took, and the enemy counter-attacks | Defend the foothold for one song | Attacker story, defender play. Players may expect to choose where to strike |
| Attacker | You send the army; the enemy builds towers | Choose and time sends | A different genre; loses mazing (pillar 1) |

- The band plays the king through each stop, so the song being the level has a story reason.
- A camp sits naturally in the middle with enemies from several sides: the centre keep map from playtest 4.
- Foothold is the middle road if the pilgrimage feels too passive.

**The night watch** (Jared's doc comment; Claude's favourite so far): you are not the king but the overworked guy on night watch. The caravan travels by day; each night it camps, everyone else sleeps, and you set up defences and keep them alive until dawn. The camp is what you defend.

- Levels are nights; the day's travel is the map change; the morning is the card pick.
- The music is what keeps you awake: one song, one night, the song ends at sunrise.
- A tired, grumbling, relatable hero gives humour and a face (the Conductor character) with no lore to explain.
- Bosses are things that hunt in the dark (the Peeker at the edge of the firelight).

**The story** (Jared): one overarching story links the campaigns. The caravan travels for some reason, but the watchman tells it, an unreliable narrator: details intentionally wrong, contradicting, or not adding up from his perspective. Running joke: "Hey, I just work here."

## 7. Open questions (Claude's lean in brackets)

1. How many levels in a campaign? (8 to 10 songs, about 20 to 25 minutes; field bosses from level 2 or 3, stage bosses placed per campaign, e.g. level 5 on a ~3:00 song; the final stage is the ultimate boss.) **Jared's call:** a campaign is one journey from one destination to another; it opens by showing how many nights of travel lie ahead (= level count). Multiple campaigns; the demo is one campaign on today's music. Length to be set by Jared (the final stage may use several songs).
2. How many cards per level? (One pick from three; a rarer draft after a boss level.)
3. How is a build window signalled? (Lull markers on a song progress bar, a 4-beat warning and "Build" flash at a lull, "Incoming" 4 beats before it ends.) **Jared's direction:** the game tells you, not the UI: drums drop out and the mix calms (a sound, a feeling of relief) for a lull; drums return and tension builds before a push; the watchman as a talking head tips the first few times only. Pillar 3. UI markers only as a test fallback.
4. Is the count-off always on? (Only for songs with no intro.) **Jared's call:** always on; the count-off is the build phase, meant to be quick.
5. How do pilgrimage map changes connect to build time? (Fresh map each level, rebuild from scratch; gold, cards and talents carry over.) **Jared's call:** new camp = new location, layout and spawns; towers left behind, no packed towers. Watch the economy if gold carries over (a tax or supply cost per night are options). Return journey past old camps parked in the backlog.
6. What happens to endless mode? (Its own mode: one map, playlist on loop, harder each lap; unlocked by finishing the journey.) **Jared's call:** the final boss shows a success card offering to stay and fight in endless; endless left out of the demo, no endless balancing now.
7. Does multiplayer use songs as levels? (Not yet; keep waves until single player proves the model.) **Jared's call:** no; multiplayer follows Legion TD more closely (downtime after a wave, sends) and gets its own discussion doc (T-025).
8. First test? (One prototype level on Night Life, 2:57, with a hand-written section script, to answer whether a 3:00 level stays exciting. Campaign levels are curated by hand, waveform only as a first draft.) **Jared's call:** Claude provides the level template and tools; Jared choreographs each level, tests and gives feedback; Claude suggests, playtests decide. So level section scripts should be plain files Jared can edit without code.
