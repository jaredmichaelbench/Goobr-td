## The core joke

Something enormous is happening somewhere else, and you will never see it. You are the night watchman of a caravan, and you have one job: keep the camp alive until dawn.

- **Locked:** you are the overworked guy on night watch, not the hero; the caravan travels by day, everyone else sleeps, and you hold the camp until sunrise (D-0048, journey doc section 5).
- **Locked:** the watchman tells the story as an unreliable narrator. Details are wrong, contradict each other, or don't add up from his view. Running line: "Hey, I just work here" (D-0048, D-0050 item 7).
- **Working:** the music is what keeps him awake. One song, one night; the song ends at sunrise (journey doc section 5).

**Proposed rules of the joke** (Working, for you to keep or cut):

1. The epic never appears on screen in full. A glimpse at most: a light on the horizon, a shape crossing the moon.
2. Every hint arrives second-hand: overheard, half-read, misremembered, or guessed.
3. No hint is fully trustworthy, the watchman included.
4. What attacks the camp is real. Everything past the firelight is rumour.
5. The watchman never gets promoted into the epic. The joke dies the moment he matters.

**Prompt: does the player ever learn the truth?**

| Option | What happens | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Never | The epic stays rumour forever; players piece it together and argue | Strongest joke, a fan-theory engine. Some players want closure |
| B. Anticlimax | The last night ends and someone tells him how it all turned out, in one bored line | A punchline ending; keeps him unimportant |
| C. He mattered all along | The watchman turns out to be key to the epic | A big twist, but it breaks rule 5; save it for a sequel if ever |

Claude's lean: A for the demo, B for the full game's final campaign.

**Jared's notes:** Option A, B could be the move but I feel like that's far off. Let people speculate. (Comments: yes to second-hand hints; even dialog and mentions in multiplayer and other modes could slowly build on the vague story. "What attacks the camp is real": correct.)

## Unreliable hints

Hints should drift in and out through things the game already has, so story costs almost no new systems. Each hint is one line or one glimpse, never a cutscene.

| Channel | Already in the game? | Example hint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watchman tips | Yes, one line over the board (D-0050) | "Heard the king's dead. Or married. One of those." |
| The morning card | Yes, card pick each dawn (D-0048) | Flavour text on the card, overheard while packing up |
| Night results screen | Yes (D-0065) | A line in the log: "Rider passed at 03:12. Didn't stop." |
| The sky and horizon | Partly, the night lighting (D-0060) | A flash, a shape across the moon, smoke at dawn |
| Enemy props | Partly, weapons and shields per type (D-0060) | A banner on a shield that matches nothing in the caravan |
| Camp chatter | No | Muffled talk from a tent during a lull |
| The journey map | No; the night count is shown (D-0048) | The route redrawn, a town crossed out |
| Random voice lines | No (your comment) | Muttered mid-fight, never repeated the same way twice |
| Level intros and starts | No (your comment) | A line as the caravan pulls into a new camp, before the count-off |
| Multiplayer and other modes | No (your comment) | Watchmen from other caravans swap rumours, each wrong in a new way |

**Three kinds of unreliable** (Working)

1. **Misheard:** the watchman gets the words wrong ("the Dragon Queen" becomes "the Drag-on Clean").
2. **Contradicted:** a later hint undoes an earlier one, and neither is marked as wrong.
3. **Biased:** he reads everything through his own job ("The war's going badly. You can tell, the rations are smaller").

**Truth ledger.** Keep a private table of every hint: what's said, what's true, what it contradicts. It stops the unreliable from becoming accidental.

## Tone and voice

Workplace comedy at night, played deadpan. The danger is real and the camp can fall; the jokes come from the watchman's attitude, never from making the fights silly.

- **Locked:** tips are one line, said once (D-0050).
- **Working:** talents and cards get renamed in this voice (backlog, name pass).
- **Touchstones (Working):** Rosencrantz and Guildenstern Are Dead (minor characters while the real play happens offstage), Star Trek: Lower Decks (the crew who aren't on the bridge), Discworld's City Watch (tired, underpaid, brave by accident).

**Sample lines to react to** (keep, cut, or rewrite)

- First lull: "Drums stopped. That means build something. Or it means nothing. I just work here."
- First parry: "Not paid enough for that."
- Dawn: "Sun's up. Nobody saw. Nobody ever sees."
- After a loss: "I'll put it in the report as 'weather'."

**Prompts**

- How dark can it get? Is anyone in the caravan ever hurt, or only the camp's health?
- Does he talk to the creatures, to himself, or to the player?
