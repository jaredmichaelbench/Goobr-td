# Rhythm vs. tower defense: where M5's playtest leaves us

_Written 2026-10-25 after Jared's M5 playtest. Discussion first, build after agreement (INDEX rule 6)._

## What the playtest said

- Eyes glued to the sequence bar. The build phase feels irrelevant: maze length is king, spend all gold on length, then you're locked into timings.
- Fear of missing a note dominates; a lock is debilitating.
- Boss: notes and parries compete for the same attention. Parries should be the fight; music after the stagger.
- Not sold on music as the centre. Rhythm should amplify the TD; at heart it must be a TD.

## My read: we drifted from pillar 2, not from the music

Pillar 2 says inputs are **punctuation, not constant drumming**. Each M3 pass made notes denser and more visible so they could be learned (v1 too dense, v2 too sparse and random, v3 the chart, v4 the timeline). By wave 12 a wave has 80+ notes with a miss penalty. That is a rhythm game with a maze under it, which is exactly what Jared is reacting to. The music itself is not the problem; the mandatory, continuous, punished note stream is.

"Maze length is king" is partly a TD truth (more time in range is always good) and partly our economy: only two tower types, no upgrades, no draft, and the Resonance bonus scales with time-in-range. TD depth is M6 content (T-019 economy, third tower, upgrades, draft), not a rhythm fix.

## Proposals

1. **Notes become punctuation.** A handful per wave (about one accent per phrase, on the bar's last beat), never a constant stream. A miss costs nothing but the bonus. The timeline goes off by default (L still toggles it for comparison); the tower ring and the music are the cue.
2. **Defense is parried, not charted.** Space becomes the one defensive rhythm input everywhere. Elites get a telegraphed pulse like the boss's bolts (wind-up, tone, on the beat); Space on the beat deflects it, a miss locks the towers in its reach. Locks come only from unparried attacks, never from a missed note. Elites are rare, so parries stay punctuation. This keeps the M4 lock/Restore loop Jared liked and unifies it with the boss.
3. **Boss wave has no chart notes.** The fight is parries. A posture break opens Crescendo, and that's when notes come (a short burst to play). Music after the stagger, as Jared described.
4. **Then TD depth (M6):** third tower type, upgrades, the draft, economy pass. That's what makes building matter beyond length.

## Now or later?

Direction now, numbers later. 1 to 3 are small changes to what exists (chart density, a toggle default, moving the elite trigger onto Space), and M6 builds on top of them, so settling direction first is cheaper than retrofitting. Tuning (lock length, charges, windows, boss HP) waits for M6 when there is more game around it.
